## CV-COLINEARITE-001

Objective: Convert an OpenCV camera into photogrammetric interior and exterior orientation. Family: practice. Provenance: agent.

START
CV-Pilote
ID: CV-COLINEARITE-001
Question: A camera calibrated with OpenCV 4.x produces 6000 × 4000 px images with a 4.0 µm pixel pitch, $f_x=f_y=6000$ px, $(c_x,c_y)=(3010.5,1985.5)$ px and $k_1=-0.06$. For a nadir image, `solvePnP` returns $R=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1)$ and $t=(-500,300,120)$ m in a local east–north–up frame. A photogrammetric package expects: principal distance and principal point in mm (image $x$ right, $y$ up, origin at the image centre, camera looking along its $-z$ axis); the projection centre; $\omega,\varphi,\kappa$ with $R_{ph}=R_x(\omega)R_y(\varphi)R_z(\kappa)$ rotating image axes into object space; and a radial coefficient $A_1$ with $\Delta r=A_1r^3$ in mm. Give the values and the conventions you had to check.
Reponse: **Expected answer**: (1) Interior orientation: $c=6000\times0.004=24.000$ mm. Taking the image centre at OpenCV's initial principal point $((w-1)/2,(h-1)/2)=(2999.5,1999.5)$, $x'_0=(3010.5-2999.5)\times0.004=+0.044$ mm and $y'_0=-(1985.5-1999.5)\times0.004=+0.056$ mm, the $y$ sign being flipped; a package that centres at $w/2$ would give $+0.042$ and $+0.058$ mm, half a pixel apart, so its pixel-centre convention must be checked. (2) Exterior orientation: projection centre $X_0=-R^\top t=(500,300,120)$ m; the photogrammetric image axes are the OpenCV camera axes multiplied by $D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1)$, so $R_{ph}=R^\top D=I$ and $\omega=\varphi=\kappa=0$, as expected for a nadir image; reading angles straight off the OpenCV matrix would give $\omega=180°$. (3) Distortion: OpenCV's $k_1$ acts on normalized coordinates and distorts ideal points, so $A_1=k_1/c^2=-0.06/576\approx-1.04\times10^{-4}$ mm$^{-2}$ if the package also adds distortion to the projected point; if it applies corrections to measured coordinates, the sign reverses to first order.

**Further explanation**: The collinearity equations and $K[R\mid t]$ describe the same central projection. Dividing camera coordinates by depth gives normalized coordinates; multiplying them by $c$ in mm instead of $f_x$ in pixels gives image coordinates in mm, which is why $c=f_x\cdot$pitch and why $k_1$ is divided by $c^2$. The matrix $D$ is a 180° rotation about $x$: it flips $y$ and $z$ together and keeps the frame right-handed. In the question's convention the package's matrix is $R^\top D$; a package whose matrix rotates object space into the image, as in Fraser's form of the collinearity equations, needs its transpose $DR$.

**Checks before trusting the numbers**: project a few object points through both models and compare pixels after converting mm back with the same centre and $y$ flip; the reference's tilted example agrees to $10^{-8}$ px. Check the sign of $c$, because some photogrammetric software stores a negative principal distance. Check names and scales: Metashape's $P_1$ multiplies the term OpenCV calls $p_2$, its $c_x$ is an offset from the image centre (here +11 px), and decentring coefficients scale with $1/c$, not $1/c^2$.

**Blocking errors**: Taking $t$ as the camera position. Converting $f_x$ to mm without the pixel pitch. Keeping OpenCV's $y$ direction for $y'_0$. Reading $\omega,\varphi,\kappa$ directly off the OpenCV rotation. Copying $k_1$ unchanged into a millimetre model or ignoring the direction of the distortion model.
Source: [[150-photogrammetrie/references/151-colinearite-autocalibration|Full reference]]; [OpenCV 4.13.0, calib3d.hpp L56–L262: K[R|t], pixel units, distortion of normalized coordinates](https://github.com/opencv/opencv/blob/4.13.0/modules/calib3d/include/opencv2/calib3d.hpp#L56-L262); [OpenCV 4.13.0, calibration.cpp L80–L81: initial centre (w−1)/2](https://github.com/opencv/opencv/blob/4.13.0/modules/calib3d/src/calibration.cpp#L80-L81); [Hastedt and Luhmann 2015, §2.1 eq. 1 and §2.2 Table 1: collinearity form and conversion scheme](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XL-1-W4/321/2015/isprsarchives-XL-1-W4-321-2015.pdf); [MicMac documentation at 501444e, §14.3.6: omega-phi-kappa matrix](https://github.com/micmacIGN/Documentation/blob/501444edf00862939b400b1f7096cd6ea87c8e8a/DocMicMac.pdf); [Metashape 2.2 manual, Appendix D, p. 242–243](https://www.agisoft.com/pdf/metashape-pro_2_2_en.pdf). Values, decentring scaling and the reprojection check: project derivations, recomputed in the check script.

## CV-COLINEARITE-002

Objective: Explain why principal distance and depth scale separate only with convergent geometry. Family: reasoning. Provenance: agent.

START
CV-Pilote
ID: CV-COLINEARITE-002
Question: For a self-calibration, a flat wall carrying targets is photographed with every optical axis perpendicular to it: most images from 2000 mm, some from 1800 and 2200 mm, several rotated by 90° about the optical axis. The principal distance (nominal 24 mm) is free and the scale comes from a calibrated bar lying on the wall. Runs started from different initial values return $c$ between 23.8 and 24.3 mm with identical residuals. (a) Compute the image coordinate of a target 500 mm off-axis for $c=24$ mm at 2000 mm, and find another (principal distance, distance) pair that gives the same value. (b) Explain why neither the varied distances, the rolls nor the bar fixed $c$, and what a wrong $c$ does to the reconstruction. (c) Which acquisition change would fix it?
Reponse: **Expected answer**: (1) $x=c\,\Delta X/H=24\times500/2000=6.000$ mm, and $c=24.24$ mm at 2020 mm also gives 6.000 mm. With parallel axes every image coordinate depends on $c$ divided by the depth along the common axis, so multiplying $c$, every camera distance and every object depth by the same factor leaves all observations unchanged: an exact null direction of the normal equations, not noise. (2) Varied distances and rolls about the axis go along with that stretch (the distances are scaled too, and rolls keep the axes parallel), and a bar on the wall measures only lateral lengths. A wrong $c$ stretches all depths by the same factor: camera distances and relief are 1% off for a 1% error in $c$ (a 400 mm relief becomes 404 mm), while lateral coordinates stay correct. (3) Make the depth directions differ between images with convergent, tilted views; alternatively add a known length with a depth component, or fix $c$ from a separate calibration at the same focus. In a constructed 8-image network, $\sigma(c)$ was 17.8 µm at 5° convergence, 2.69 µm at 15° and 0.84 µm at 35°, and rolls did not reduce it.

**Further explanation**: For an image looking along $-z$ from height $Z_0$, the collinearity equation gives $x=-c\,p_x/p_z$ with $p_z=Z-Z_0$. Replace $c$ by $kc$, every $Z_0$ by $kZ_0$ and every object $Z$ by $kZ$, keeping $X$ and $Y$: the numerator is unchanged, the denominator gains $k$, and $k$ cancels. A roll mixes $p_x$ and $p_y$ but never $p_z$, so it cannot help. With GNSS-measured camera positions, as in aerial blocks, two flying heights would instead separate $c$ from a constant height offset. This is the network version of the single-view $(f,Z)$ coupling of CV-SVD.

**What relief can and cannot do**: a target 400 mm in front of the wall images at 7.5 mm in both solutions if its relief is estimated, because it is stretched to 404 mm. Only if its 400 mm offset is known does the second solution move it by −18.5 µm, about 4.6 px at 4 µm, which the adjustment can detect. Convergent views break the stretch because each image has its own depth direction. Remondino and Fraser ask for "the maximum possible imaging scale variation throughout the image format"; Triggs et al. suggest views on a 30–45° cone for the self-calibration of a flat object.

**Blocking errors**: Blaming solver noise or damping for a geometric null space. Expecting more parallel images, more distances or more rolls to fix $c$. Concluding that the lateral bar validates depths. Keeping the run with the lowest residual.
Source: [[150-photogrammetrie/references/151-colinearite-autocalibration|Full reference]]; [Remondino and Fraser 2006, §5: convergence, rolls and scale variation for planar arrays](https://www.isprs.org/PROCEEDINGS/XXXVI/part5/paper/REMO_616.pdf); [Triggs et al. 2000, §11: 30–45° cone for self-calibration](https://hal.science/inria-00548290/document); [Hastedt and Luhmann 2015, §4: rolled and tilted images in a simulated block](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XL-1-W4/321/2015/isprsarchives-XL-1-W4-321-2015.pdf). Invariance, numbers and the network study: project derivation and constructed covariance analysis, recomputed in the check script.

## CV-COLINEARITE-003

Objective: Diagnose principal-point coupling from standard deviations and correlations. Family: diagnosis. Provenance: agent.

START
CV-Pilote
ID: CV-COLINEARITE-003
Question: Two free-network self-calibrations of the same 24 mm camera use the same flat target array, the same eight stations on a 15° cone and the same seven parameters ($c$, $x_p$, $y_p$, two radial terms, $P_1$, $P_2$), with an image standard deviation of 0.6 µm. Network A keeps every image in landscape orientation; network B rotates the camera by 0°, 90°, 180° and 270° about its axis. Both fit with the same residual RMS. A reports $\sigma(x_p)=15.2$ µm, a largest correlation of $x_p$ with an exterior parameter of 0.997 and $\rho(x_p,P_1)=-0.05$; B reports 1.0 µm, 0.79 and $+0.82$. A colleague keeps A because its principal point is "decorrelated from decentring". Diagnose both networks and say what you would check before using B's calibration in another project.
Reponse: **Expected answer**: (1) A is the defective calibration: its principal point is coupled with the exterior orientation. Without rolls, a principal-point shift is absorbed by rotating the images and moving the free object points, so many principal points fit the same residuals; $\sigma(x_p)=15.2$ µm (about 2.5 px at 6 µm) says it is not determined, and the low $\rho(x_p,P_1)$ only shows that $x_p$ is tied to something else. (2) In B, the rolls break this coupling and divide $\sigma(x_p)$ by about 15. The remaining $\rho(x_p,P_1)$ is the expected coupling between principal point and decentring: a shifted distortion centre is, to first order, a decentring term ($B_1=-A_1e$). It grows with focal length and is reduced by object depth and stronger convergence, not by rolls. (3) Before transferring B: test the significance and stability of $P_1,P_2$ and of the principal point across subsets (for instance per roll angle), read the whole correlation matrix rather than one pair, and check independent lengths in object space. Do not drop $P_1,P_2$ merely to lower a correlation: if real decentring is removed, the principal point absorbs it.

**Further explanation**: Project explanation of the coupling: near the image centre a constant image shift resembles a small camera rotation, $\delta x\approx c\,\delta\varphi$. When all images share the same roll, a principal-point shift corresponds to rotations about the same object-space axis in every image, which the free object points can follow. After a 90° roll, the same sensor shift needs a rotation about another object axis, and the images disagree. Remondino and Fraser found a planar network without rolls "essentially singular": "any plausible value for the principal point offset" fitted with an RMSE of 0.57 to 0.63 µm, and including $P_1,P_2$ made no difference. With the target coordinates fixed instead of free, the same constructed comparison changed $\sigma(x_p)$ only from 1.08 to 0.99 µm.

**Reading correlations**: a correlation coefficient is one entry of the covariance matrix; a dependency spread over many parameters can inflate a standard deviation while every pairwise coefficient stays moderate. Read standard deviations, the full correlation matrix and subset stability together. OpenCV `calibrateCamera` returns standard deviations but no correlations; Metashape's report includes a correlation matrix. Hastedt and Luhmann found that omitting decentring changed the principal point significantly for one camera.

**Blocking errors**: Preferring A because one correlation is low. Treating equal residual RMS as proof of an equally good principal point. Expecting rolls to remove the decentring correlation. Dropping $P_1,P_2$ without a significance test.
Source: [[150-photogrammetrie/references/151-colinearite-autocalibration|Full reference]]; [Remondino and Fraser 2006, §4.2 (decentring coupling), §5 (orthogonal rolls) and §6.2 (planar network without rolls)](https://www.isprs.org/PROCEEDINGS/XXXVI/part5/paper/REMO_616.pdf); [Hastedt and Luhmann 2015, §3.3: principal point and decentring correlation](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XL-1-W4/321/2015/isprsarchives-XL-1-W4-321-2015.pdf); [OpenCV 4.13.0, calib3d.hpp L1640–L1643: standard deviations only](https://github.com/opencv/opencv/blob/4.13.0/modules/calib3d/include/opencv2/calib3d.hpp#L1640-L1643). Network numbers: constructed covariance analysis; coupling mechanism and decentring equivalence: project derivations, recomputed in the check script.

## CV-COLINEARITE-004

Objective: Design a self-calibrating close-range network with scale and acceptance checks. Family: design. Provenance: agent.

START
CV-Pilote
ID: CV-COLINEARITE-004
Question: You must measure about 100 retro-reflective targets on a welded frame of 2.0 × 1.0 × 0.5 m with a maximum length measurement error of 0.05 mm. The camera is one DSLR with a 24 mm lens and a 36 × 24 mm sensor, used at about 2 m, and its calibration will be estimated in the same free-network bundle. Specify the image network, the target and scale layout and the acceptance test, and state the point precision the bundle must report.
Reponse: **Expected answer**: (1) Geometry: convergent stations all around the frame at two or more heights (for example 12 stations × 2 heights, adjacent viewing directions about 30° apart), with four images rolled by 0°, 90°, 180° and 270° at each station, 96 images in all; every target seen in at least 4 and preferably about 8 images; targets covering the whole image format; focus fixed and recorded; coded targets for automatic orientation. (2) Scale and checks: two or three calibrated scale bars in different directions, one with a depth component, used as constraints, plus several independent calibrated lengths, not used in the adjustment, spread through the volume in several orientations; stable temperature and certified lengths. (3) Acceptance: the bundle must report $s_{XYZ}\le0.05/\sqrt{18}\approx0.012$ mm per coordinate, since the theoretical length error is $\sqrt{18}\,s_{XYZ}$; the independent lengths must then give $LME_{max}=\max|l_{measured}-l_{calibrated}|\le0.05$ mm, with additional-parameter significance, correlations and residual maps checked. Passing the precision test but failing the length test indicates a systematic error, not noise.

**Further explanation**: Each element breaks a specific ambiguity. Convergence separates principal distance from depth, rolls separate principal point from exterior orientation, depth and convergence reduce the principal point–decentring coupling, and full-format coverage constrains radial terms at the edges. Remondino and Fraser make highly convergent images, orthogonal roll angles and many well-distributed points the condition for reliable self-calibration; precision grows with the square root of the number of rays (a factor $\sqrt2\approx1.41$ from 4 to 8) and levels off near 8 rays. Triggs et al. plan at least four images per point for reliability and name covariance estimation of the planned network as the basic quantitative design aid, so simulate the network before acquisition.

**Practice from VDI/VDE 2634 tests**: Reznicek et al. used about 160 images from 12 positions at three levels with four rolled images per position, coded and uncoded retro-targets and 58 calibrated lengths on seven multi-scale bars. Hastedt et al. used 144 images and three extra scale bars along the main axes as scale conditions, with the longest test length about two-thirds of the volume diagonal. These are reported setups, not minima.

**Sizing check**: at 2000 mm the lens images 3000 × 2000 mm (scale 1:83), so the frame fills most of the format in both roll orientations; moving closer improves image scale only while the object still covers the rolled images.

**Blocking errors**: Parallel views of each face. Omitting rolled images. Scaling and checking with the same bar. Accepting on $\hat\sigma_0$ or point precision alone. Refocusing between images.
Source: [[150-photogrammetrie/references/151-colinearite-autocalibration|Full reference]]; [Remondino and Fraser 2006, §5: network rules](https://www.isprs.org/PROCEEDINGS/XXXVI/part5/paper/REMO_616.pdf); [Triggs et al. 2000, §11: network design](https://hal.science/inria-00548290/document); [Reznicek et al. 2016, §2–§2.2: VDI 2634 test setup](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLI-B5/107/2016/isprs-archives-XLI-B5-107-2016.pdf); [Hastedt et al. 2018, §3.1–§3.3 and eq. 2: scale bars, network, theoretical LME](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLII-2/441/2018/isprs-archives-XLII-2-441-2018.pdf); [Fraser 2006, §3.4 and §4: coded targets](https://www.isprs.org/proceedings/XXXVI/part5/paper/FRAS_611.pdf). Design numbers: project calculations, recomputed in the check script.

## CV-COLINEARITE-005

Objective: Separate internal precision from external accuracy with independent check lengths. Family: evaluation. Provenance: agent.

START
CV-Pilote
ID: CV-COLINEARITE-005
Question: A self-calibrating bundle of a 2 m object, scaled by one 1000 mm bar, reports an a posteriori $\hat\sigma_0$ consistent with the a priori 0.05 px and a mean point precision of 0.010 mm per coordinate, about 1:115,000 of the object size. Four independent calibrated bars of 1500, 1200, 800 and 500 mm are measured 0.105, 0.086, 0.054 and 0.036 mm too long. Is the system as accurate as its precision suggests? Quantify, diagnose and say what should be reported.
Reponse: **Expected answer**: (1) No. Precision predicts a length standard deviation near $\sqrt2\times0.010=0.014$ mm and a theoretical length error $\sqrt{18}\times0.010\approx0.042$ mm; the observed maximum, 0.105 mm, is 2.5 times larger, and all errors share one sign and grow with length. A fit gives about +70 ppm with a near-zero intercept: a scale error of about 1:14,000. (2) $\hat\sigma_0$ and the covariance measure the internal consistency of image residuals under the adopted model and datum, that is precision; they cannot see a wrong scale constraint (certificate value, temperature, offsets of the bar's targets) or an unmodelled systematic effect. Comparison with reference lengths measures accuracy, in the VIM sense of closeness to a true value. (3) Report the maximum and RMS length errors and the error-versus-length trend from lengths that did not enter the adjustment, next to the precision; find the scale cause, use several bars in different orientations, and validate any scale correction on new independent lengths.

**Further explanation**: Two independent points with 0.010 mm per coordinate give a length standard deviation near $\sqrt2\times0.010$ mm; three times that is the $\sqrt{18}$ rule used by Hastedt et al., who obtain 21 and 34 µm for 5 and 8 µm point precision. The rule bounds random error only. Errors proportional to length point to scale: removing the 70.5 ppm trend leaves residuals within 2.1 µm, consistent with the precision. With one bar defining the scale, its error passes unchanged into every length and into no image residual.

**Evidence from the sources**: in a 28 m free network, Hastedt et al. report an RMS point precision of 74.4 µm (1:376,000) together with a maximum length error of −2.27 mm, all distances too short, and cite relative accuracy up to 100% lower than relative precision. Reznicek et al. separated a 40 µm maximum length error from a 4 µm random component by repeating a VDI/VDE 2634 test ten times. Remondino and Fraser treat object-point RMSE, not image residuals, as the main quality indicator of a calibration.

**Vocabulary**: the VIM defines accuracy as closeness to a true value and precision as closeness between replicate measurements; Triggs et al. note that photogrammetry sometimes says "precision" where they say "accuracy". State the definition with each number.

**Blocking errors**: Quoting 1:115,000 as accuracy. Using the adjustment's scale bar as a check. Averaging signed errors towards zero. Rescaling by 70 ppm and declaring success on the same four bars.
Source: [[150-photogrammetrie/references/151-colinearite-autocalibration|Full reference]]; [JCGM 200:2012 (VIM), 2.13–2.15: accuracy, trueness, precision](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM_200_2012.pdf); [Hastedt et al. 2018, §1, §3.3 eq. 2, §4.3 Tables 5–6](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLII-2/441/2018/isprs-archives-XLII-2-441-2018.pdf); [Reznicek et al. 2016, §2.4 and §3.1](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLI-B5/107/2016/isprs-archives-XLI-B5-107-2016.pdf); [Remondino and Fraser 2006, §6.1](https://www.isprs.org/PROCEEDINGS/XXXVI/part5/paper/REMO_616.pdf); [Triggs et al. 2000, §10, footnote 20](https://hal.science/inria-00548290/document). Check-length numbers: constructed, recomputed in the check script.
